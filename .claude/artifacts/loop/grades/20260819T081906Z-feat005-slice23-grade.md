## What went well

Verifying each of the 3 planned deletion targets against live consumers before deleting turned a naive 3-target deletion into 1 safe deletion (approvals.mts, 297 LOC, zero live consumers) plus 2 correctly-blocked targets — claims.mts stayed because quickwin.md's documented workflow depends on it, and auto-dispatch.mts stayed because it's wired into 7+ call sites plus two live analytics readers the plan never accounted for.

## What went wrong

The plan's cut list confused a subsystem name with an unrelated live one: `improve-cleanup.md`/`improve-rewrite-today.md`'s dispatch-metrics naming collided with the hierarchical-dispatch/wave swarm telemetry subsystem, which has nothing to do with the actually-targeted auto-dispatch.jsonl.

## What I would do differently next time

Treat plan text naming a deletion target as a hypothesis to verify against the current call graph, not a fact to execute — improvement-review findings age, and a similarly-named subsystem is an easy trap for a blind mechanical deletion.
